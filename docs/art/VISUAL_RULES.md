# Visual Rules

Quick reference for artists and designers. When in doubt, choose **less light** and **more space**.

---

## Do

- Use **dark greys, cold blues, muted steel**
- Keep walkways **narrow** and pits **open**
- Let fog and gradient sell infinite depth
- Use **silhouettes** and partial reveals
- Contrast **tiny player** vs **large environment**
- Allow **empty** rooms and long sightlines into black
- Keep combat VFX **subtle** and desaturated
- Hide the pit floor when possible

---

## Do Not

- Flood arenas with warm orange fill lights
- Use saturated cartoon particles
- Show a clear, safe ground plane in the abyss
- Over-decorate every surface with props
- Fully light enemies in the pit
- Use comedy gore or bright blood geysers
- Reveal void “monsters” clearly in full light
- Add busy UI chrome that breaks isolation

---

## Void Visual Rules

- Void = **gradient + motion + mystery**, not a texture on a quad
- Moving fog layers below playable space
- Drifting particles suggest something alive
- Distant shapes **optional**, never mandatory in combat reads
- Void deaths: stylized corruption / disintegration, not realistic anatomy

---

## Darkness Rules

- If the player can see the bottom, tension drops
- Hard shadows on deck; soft falloff into pit
- Information in darkness is **withheld on purpose**
- Player flashlight (if added later) must not trivialize pits

---

## Scale Rules

- Player ~ human scale; environment **oversized**
- Vertical drops feel long — exaggerate fall time before score
- Props on bridge small; pillars and void volume **large**

---

## Readability Rules

- Walkable floor always readable
- Enemy vs player distinguishable at combat range
- Weapon feedback clear (projectiles, knockback)
- HUD health/shield legible without breaking mood
- Pit edges must be **fair** — player understands “I can fall here”
